# Trade outreach — funeral directors (A4)

Companion to `01-funeral-directors-list.md`. The approach, not just the list.

## The core argument: never lead with price

Bluwave cannot win a price war against a printer in a cheaper region — London rent and London
wages make that structural, not negotiable. Matching on price donates margin and teaches a
price-shopper to keep pushing.

**What can't be shipped from 250 miles away is proximity and speed.**

A funeral date cannot move. The details change late — a corrected name, an added reader, a
changed hymn on the Tuesday for a Thursday service. When that happens, a printer two hours up the
motorway cannot help, and it is the **funeral director's** reputation with a grieving family that
is on the line, not the printer's.

So the reply to "you need to lower your prices" is not a number. It's a question:

> *"What happens when the family changes the order of service on the Tuesday for a Thursday
> funeral?"*

That reframes the conversation from unit price to risk — which is the only ground Bluwave wins on.

## What to offer instead of a discount

All cheaper to give than price, and all things a distant competitor structurally cannot match:

- **Guaranteed turnaround** — the site already promises 24–48 hours and a same-day quote
- **Free late amendments** up to a stated cutoff
- **A priority slot** for order-of-service work
- **Trade proofing** — a proof before anything runs
- **Local collection or delivery** — no courier risk on an immovable date

## If volume is offered in exchange for price

It can be legitimate, but test it before agreeing:

1. **Is there genuinely idle press capacity?** Marginal cost on digital is mostly materials. But
   if the press is busy, cheap volume costs you the better job you turned away.
2. **Is the volume committed, with the rate stepping down only at thresholds actually reached?**
   "I'll send you more work" is not a commitment.
3. **Run the arithmetic.** At roughly 30% margin, a 15% price cut halves gross margin — you need
   about *double* the volume simply to stand still. If they can't commit to that, the discount is
   a loss wearing the costume of a win.
4. **Trade only.** A director supplying clean artwork and a correct running order is cheap to
   serve. Discounted volume on hand-held family work is actively worse, because that consumes
   hours, not just materials.

## Why trade work is worth chasing beyond the revenue

Every friction in family funeral work — low-resolution photos, not knowing what the running order
should be, having to phone the priest for details — is a **first-timer problem**. A funeral
director already knows the running order, already has the officiant's details, and supplies usable
artwork because they do this every week.

So trade work is better revenue *and* less of the work that's least enjoyable. That's why this is
the priority item, not just another lead source.

## Qualifying questions for a first conversation

- Who prints your orders of service now, and where are they based?
- What's your typical turnaround, and has a late change ever caught you out?
- How many services a week do you handle?
- Do you design the order of service yourself or does the family supply it?
- Would a trade account with proofing and a priority slot be useful?

That fourth question matters most: if they design in-house, they're cheap to serve and worth
pursuing hard. If they hand it off, the job carries the same time cost as family work.

## Draft opening email

> **Subject: Order of service printing — Beckenham, 24–48 hours**
>
> Hello [name],
>
> I'm [name] from Bluwave, a print shop in Beckenham. We've been printing in south east London
> since 2004 and we do a lot of order of service and memorial work.
>
> I'm not writing about price. I'm writing because of what happens when a service order changes
> late. We're local, we turn work round in 24–48 hours, and we can take an amendment on the
> Tuesday for a Thursday funeral — which a printer further afield generally can't.
>
> If it would be useful, I'd be glad to set up a trade account with proofing and a priority slot
> for order-of-service work, and to quote a sample job so you can see the quality and the timing.
>
> Would a short call suit you this week or next?
>
> [name], Bluwave Ltd, Beckenham — [phone] — [email]
> *If you'd rather not hear from us again, just reply and we'll remove you.*

## Compliance

- **Cold calls: screen against CTPS first.** Legal requirement for unsolicited B2B calls.
- **Cold email:** permitted to corporate subscribers (limited companies, LLPs) with a working
  opt-out on every message. Sole traders and individuals need consent — and many small funeral
  directors *are* sole traders, so check the trading entity before emailing.
- Keep a record of who has asked not to be contacted, and honour it across both channels.

## What to measure

Contacted / replied / call booked / quoted / won / value. Six columns, one spreadsheet. If twenty
approaches produce no quotes, the pitch is wrong — change it rather than sending more.
