# Note for Dad — five things, about an hour and a half total

*(Forwardable as-is. Deliberately short — the whole point is that it gets done.)*

---

Hi Dad,

You mentioned wanting to know what people are interested in so you could run campaigns off it.
I've looked into doing that from the website, and honestly it won't work — the site only gets
about 80 visitors a week, and most of your enquiries come by email and phone anyway, so the
website only ever sees a small slice of what's really going on.

The better version is much simpler: **the best campaign you can run is ringing customers who
already buy from you and have gone quiet.** That's the cheapest revenue there is, and everything
needed for it is already in Sage.

To build that list I need three things from you.

## 1. A sales export from Sage — about 15 minutes

A list of **sales by customer for the last 3 years**, with just three columns:

- customer name
- invoice date
- amount

Exported as a CSV or Excel file. In Sage this is usually under Reports → Customer / Sales, called
something like "Sales by Customer" or "Customer Activity" — the exact name depends on which
version you're on. No need for line-by-line detail on what was printed, just who and when.

**What I'll do with it:** work out, for each customer, how often they normally order and how long
it's been. Someone who usually orders every four months and hasn't been in touch for eleven is
worth a phone call. You'd get back a ranked list — most overdue first, with what they last spent
and when.

How long that list runs I don't know yet, and I'd rather not invent a number: it depends on how
many named accounts you've got, which is exactly what question 2 tells me.

## 2. One quick check in Sage — about 10 minutes

Roughly how much of your sales over that period went to the **miscellaneous account** (the online
and card-machine takings), versus customers with their own named accounts?

I don't need exact figures, just the rough split. It tells me whether the list above covers most
of the business or only a corner of it, before I build anything on top of it.

## 3. A conversation — about 20 minutes

**Five jobs you quoted and didn't win, and why you think you lost them.**

This one isn't in Sage — a quote that never became an order leaves no trace in the accounts. It's
only in your head. It doesn't need to be a proper list or complete, just five you remember.

It's the most useful of the three, because "we lose on price" and "we lose on turnaround" point in
completely different directions.

## And one for the accountant, whenever

Which of your product categories are **zero-rated for VAT** and which are standard-rated? I noticed
a booklet job came through with no VAT, which is right for booklets — but business cards, posters
and calendars are treated differently. We need to know before deciding how prices are shown on the
website, or some of them will be misleading.

## One thing I'd flag separately

The two funeral directors closest to you in Beckenham are both chain-owned (Co-op and Dignity), so
they buy their print centrally and there's no point approaching them.

But the **independent** funeral directors around south London are a different story, and I think
they're the best opportunity going. A funeral director already knows the running order, already has
the vicar's details, and sends usable photos — so it's the same work without the parts you were
complaining about, and they come back every week rather than once.

I've started a list. That one I can get on with myself without needing anything from you.

## 4. Questions about trade work — about 20 minutes

The website reads as a shop for the public. Nothing on it tells a designer, an agency, another
printer or a funeral director that we take their work too. I've built a trade page (`/trade`) and
kept the funeral director one (`/for-funeral-directors`), but neither goes live until the
questions below have real answers. A trade customer will hold us to whatever the page says, so
I'm not guessing any of it.

**First, which trade do you mean?** Designers and agencies printing for their clients? Other
printers sending overflow? Event and wedding planners? Funeral directors? Some of those, or all
of them? And does any of this work come in already — if so, from whom?

(Councils, the NHS, charities and schools aren't on the trade page any more — they buy for
themselves rather than for a client, so they've got their own page now. See section 5.)

Then seven answers, all things you already know. One answer each covers every kind of trade
customer:

1. **The cut-off** — the latest a customer can approve their proof and still hit a given day.
   Two parts:
   - What time of day does approved artwork need to reach you for the job to start that day?
     (e.g. 12pm, 2pm)
   - When is it ready if it arrives before that time? (e.g. next working day, or same day for
     short runs)

   If it depends on the job — short digital runs versus litho, say — one line for each is fine:
   "Digital: approved by 2pm, ready next working day. Litho: we'll agree a date when you order."
2. **Saturdays.** Would you work one, e.g. for a Monday funeral or a Monday event? Yes or no.
   Either is fine, but it needs to be one of them.
3. **Getting it to them.** Do they collect, or do we deliver? Is delivery charged or included?
   And would we deliver straight to *their* client?
4. **Whose name is on it.** If a designer or funeral director wants their name on the work and
   ours nowhere on it — print, packaging, delivery note — is that fine?
5. **Late changes.** A change lands the day before it's due (a name spelled wrong on Wednesday for
   a Thursday service). What actually happens?
6. **Is there a trade price?** Not the number — that stays off the website. Just whether trade
   work is priced differently from walk-in work, and on what basis: regular orders, volume, or
   them sending print-ready files.
7. **Accounts.** Account or pay per job? (More on this below — it can be decided case by case.)

**Why these and not others:** right now the site says "fast turnaround". That's an adjective,
and every printer in London uses it. A trade customer doesn't buy an adjective — they're the
ones who have to face their client if the job isn't there, so what they need is a day and a time
they can rely on. One real cut-off is worth more than a page of reassuring language.

**One last check — the artwork guidelines.** There's a new page telling customers how to set up
files. I've used the usual specs; tell me if the press wants anything different:

- 3mm bleed on every edge, text kept 5mm inside the trim
- images at 300dpi at printed size, set up in CMYK
- fonts embedded or outlined
- booklets supplied as single pages, page count in multiples of four
- large files sent as a WeTransfer / Google Drive / Dropbox link to info@

**What I'll do with it:** put the answers on the pages and switch them on. A "Trade" link then
appears in the menu and footer, on the homepage and on every product page, and the quote form
already asks "who's it for?" so trade jobs arrive marked `[TRADE]` in the email subject.

## 5. The organisations page — about 10 minutes

Your biggest named customers — the NHS, Great Ormond Street, Macmillan, Marie Curie, Southwark and
Bromley — were only on the website as a row of logos. There's now a page for them
(`/for-organisations`) and a "Business" link in the menu. Unlike the trade page it's ready to go
live as it stands, because it only says things the site already says somewhere: accounts for
regular customers, a proof before anything runs, free collection, booking press time against a
date.

To make it say more, a few quick ones:

1. **Purchase orders.** Councils and the NHS usually need a PO number on the invoice. Do you take
   those already? If so the page can say so — it's often the first thing their finance team asks.
2. **Supplier lists.** Are we on any council or NHS approved-supplier list or framework? If yes,
   that's worth naming on the page.
3. **Artwork on file.** The page says forms, letterheads and recurring leaflets are kept on file
   for reprints. Is that true across the board, or only for some customers?
4. **Who actually orders.** Which teams or departments at those organisations send the work, and
   what do they order most? It tells me which products to put first on the page.

## And a decision, when you've had a think

**Do you want to offer trade customers an account, or take payment per job?**

This one isn't a quick answer like the others above — it's a proper decision, so no rush. Thirty
days is the usual trade arrangement as far as I know, but that's my assumption rather than
something I've checked, and I'd rather you heard it from the funeral directors than from me.

The reason it may matter: a funeral director often isn't paid until the estate settles, so
paying us per job could leave them out of pocket in the meantime — which would make terms the
thing that wins the account rather than price. I can ask them directly when I ring round, and
it costs nothing to find out before you commit either way. It's your cash flow and your risk,
so it's your call.

Worth saying: we don't have to settle it to get started. The page can say "accounts available
for regular trade customers" and you decide case by case as they come.

I'm deliberately not putting trade prices on the page, by the way. Anything published there can
be read by competitors, and by families — who'd then be able to work out their funeral director's
markup, which isn't a conversation we want to hand our own customer. Prices stay in the phone
call. The page just has to make them want to make the call.
